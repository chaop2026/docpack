I'm building SafeFile, a browser-only tool that covers personal details in a document.

Public tenders here often ask you to "mask the personal information" on an insurance card. Then they stop. They don't say which numbers.

I assumed the fix was to hide more. It isn't. Hiding too much gets the paper rejected too — the reviewer needs the name and the date of birth to check who you are. There are limits on both sides, and "play it safe" is not one of them.

So the tool stopped asking "which fields?" and started asking one thing: where is this going? A tender, a bank, an AI chat. Each answer maps to a different set of bars. Same card, three different outputs.

Two things surprised me:

1. The small print matters more than the big number. A 2-digit branch code sits in its own box, away from the main number, and that's the piece people leave in.

2. A QR square can carry the number you just covered. You can't tell by looking, so the tool treats them as one thing.

It's free and still rough. If paperwork has ever come back to you for the wrong reason, try it and tell me what confused you.
