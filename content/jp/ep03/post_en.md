A renewal-fee clause in a lease, the payment date in a works contract, a deduction line on a payslip — ask an AI and you get an explanation in seconds. The convenience is real. But the text you pasted carries things the question never needed: the landlord's and guarantor's names, the other party's address and phone number, the account to pay into, your own employee number. Most AI services retain what you type, and depending on the settings it may also be used for training. There is generally no way to take it back afterwards.

Decide what the question actually needs first, and the amount you have to cover turns out to be small.

## Keep versus cover

| Keep (the AI needs it) | Cover (the question does not) |
| --- | --- |
| Clause wording, headings, article numbers | Names, addresses and phone numbers of the other party, guarantor or counterparty |
| Amounts and how they relate, payment dates | Account numbers, balances, employee numbers |
| Dates, terms, renewal conditions | Seals and signatures |
| The title that identifies the document type | Your own street-level address, if you prefer |

Keeping the clause wording is the part that matters. Anonymising "Party A shall, to Party B" changes nothing, but deleting the amounts and the dates makes the answer worse. Minimisation here means reducing the personal data without reducing the quality of the answer.

## Steps

1. Drag in the PDF or the photo of the contract.
2. Choose "Purpose: sharing for advice". The other party's name, address and account come up as candidates; the clauses and figures stay.
3. Paste the downloaded image into the AI. The output is flattened to an image, so the text under the bars cannot be copied out.

If you need to paste text rather than an image, retype it after redacting, or replace proper nouns with placeholders such as "Landlord A" or "Account X". Handing over a character string keeps the original characters no matter how it looks.

Internal policy or a client engagement may restrict entering documents into third-party services. **Always check the recipient's own instructions.** For anything work-related, check your organisation's rules before you paste.

Files are processed inside your browser and are never uploaded.
