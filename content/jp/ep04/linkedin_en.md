I'm building SafeFile, a browser-only tool that covers personal details in a document.

I had one setting for "sharing a contract to get advice". This week I found out it was wrong, in a way I did not expect.

If you paste a contract into an AI chat, you want the other party's name gone. It adds nothing to the answer.

If you send the same contract to a lawyer, covering that name can be the wrong move. They check whether the parties conflict with an existing client before they take the case. Hide it and the check cannot happen.

So the same document, the same question, two opposite answers — and the thing that decides is whether the reader is a person.

Two things I took from it:

1. "What is this for?" was not a fine enough question. The real one is closer to "who reads it, and what do they have to decide?"

2. A default that is safe in one context is a bug in another. I had treated "hide more" as the cautious choice. Here it costs the user a week.

It's free and still rough. If you advise people for a living, I'd like to know what you actually need left visible.
