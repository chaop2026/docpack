I'm building SafeFile, a browser-only tool that covers personal details in a document.

The sentence is "please apply masking as appropriate". It appears in tender notices and again in subsidy handbooks. It never says which parts.

I had been treating that as a gap to fill. Build a good enough default, cover what a careful person would cover, done.

That is wrong, and the reason is specific. These documents are being audited. A subsidy officer is checking who was paid, when, and how much. Cover the amount to be safe and the submission comes back. Cover the name and it comes back. The careful choice and the correct choice point in opposite directions.

So the tool does not guess. It asks where the document is going, shows what it would cover, and lets you put any of it back — and the article tells you to ask the desk when the handbook stops at "as appropriate".

Two things I learned:

1. A good default needs a loss function. "Hide more" only looks safe when nobody is reading the document for a decision.

2. The hard part of this product is not detection. It is knowing who is on the other end.

It's free and still rough. If you handle grant paperwork, I'd like to know what you have had sent back.
