I'm building SafeFile, a browser-only tool that covers personal details in a document.

Every design decision so far assumed one person: you have a document, you decide what to hide, you send it. This week I wrote about a case that breaks it — a family member submitting paperwork for a parent in hospital.

Now there are two people's documents in play, and one of them did not choose any of this.

Three things I had not accounted for:

1. The proxy needs to prove who they are too. The thing people forget is never the patient's card. It is their own.

2. Whether you may cover a number on someone else's document is not yours to decide. Some counters want it covered. Some want a plain copy. The holder is not in the room to ask.

3. My tool is happy to redact anything you drop into it. That is correct behaviour and it is also not the whole answer, so the article leads with "ask first" rather than with the tool.

I don't think software fixes this one. The best it can do is not pretend the question is simple.

It's free and still rough. If you have handled paperwork for a parent, I'd like to know what got sent back.
