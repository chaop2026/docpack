A hospitalised parent's paperwork, an application for a child, a spouse posted away from home. When the person cannot go to the counter, a family member submits for them. The set of documents is not the same as when the holder goes themselves.

What usually turns out to be missing is not the holder's document. It is the proxy's.

## What changes

| | The holder submits | A family member submits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Holder's ID | needed | needed |
| Proxy's ID | — | **needed** (a driving licence, say) |
| Proof of relationship | — | a copy of the residence record may be asked for |
| Letter of authority | — | depends on the recipient |
| May you redact it | your own call | **ask** — it is not your document |

Within one household, a residence record is usually enough to show the relationship; across households, a family register entry may be asked for. Which one suffices is the recipient's decision.

That last row is the hard one. Whether a family member may redact the holder's document before submitting differs by recipient. Some counters ask for the numbers covered; some ask for a plain copy of the original. Say that you are submitting on someone's behalf, and ask.

## The order to ask in

1. Tell the recipient you are submitting on behalf of someone, and get the list of documents
2. Check whether the proxy's ID and the proof of relationship are on it
3. Ask about redaction separately for the holder's documents and for yours

Keep a record of the answer; it is the input for next time.

## Why

Requirements for submission by proxy differ by scheme, and two counters in the same office can operate differently. This episode is about the order to check in, not about a particular legal requirement.
