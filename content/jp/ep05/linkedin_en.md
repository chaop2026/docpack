I'm building SafeFile, a browser-only tool that covers personal details in a document.

That report was correct, and I had assumed it could not happen. Covering something removes information, so the file should shrink.

It does not. The safe way to cover text is to flatten the page into a picture, and a picture of a page of text is bigger than the text was. A phone photo gets smaller. A clean text PDF gets larger. Same operation, opposite results.

This matters here because Japanese tender portals cap the attachment size, and the cap is set by each contracting body. I found 5MB, 10MB and 50MB in three different instruction documents. A tenth of one to another.

Two things I changed:

1. The article now tells people to redact first and check the size last. Do it the other way and you compress, then redact, then blow the cap, then redo the redaction at 11pm.

2. I stopped treating "it got smaller" as the success case. The tool reports what came out; it does not promise a direction.

It's free and still rough. If you have ever had a submission bounced on file size, I'd like to hear what the limit was.
